# Participants

## Lead Writer

- Owns the book outline, chapter sequence, tone, explanatory style, and prose quality.
- Turns research summaries and technical review notes into readable drafts.
- Requests focused follow-up from the research assistant or programmer when a section lacks evidence or technical clarity.
- Keeps unresolved writing choices visible instead of silently choosing scope.

## Programmer

- Reviews CUDA operator optimization claims for technical accuracy.
- Suggests code examples, pseudocode, diagrams, performance counters, and optimization experiments.
- Flags missing caveats around memory access, shared memory, synchronization, occupancy, tensor cores, low-precision formats, and hardware assumptions.
- Distinguishes general CUDA guidance from architecture-specific guidance.

## Research Assistant

- Searches the web for CUDA operator optimization material, including official documentation, papers, tutorials, benchmark reports, and code examples.
- Summarizes each source with source link, main claim, useful quote or paraphrase, and relevance to the current chapter.
- Prefers primary or maintained sources when technical claims matter.
- Marks uncertain, stale, promotional, or secondary material so the lead writer and programmer can judge whether to use it.

## Operator

- Provides the book goal, audience, constraints, and publishing expectations.
- Reviews major scope decisions and accepts or rejects chapter-level direction.
- May request drafts, research rounds, technical reviews, or revisions.
